Our story · since 2014

A blue door, a very hot oven, and a lot of flour

Porta Blu started as a two-person pizza counter in a former bike shop on SE Alder. Ten years on it’s a 70-seat dining room, but the recipe hasn’t changed: good flour, patience, and fire.

How it started

From a family pizzeria in Caserta to SE Alder Street

Chef Lucia Moretti learned to stretch dough at thirteen, standing on a milk crate in an aunt’s pizzeria outside Naples. Twenty years and two restaurant kitchens later, Lucia landed in Portland with a sourdough starter, a stack of handwritten recipes, and a plan to open a place that felt like the pizzerias back home: loud, warm, and a little bit messy.

The space came first. An empty bike shop with a tall arched doorway that Sam Okafor, Lucia’s business partner, painted cobalt blue the week they signed the lease. Neighbors started giving directions by it (“the blue door on Alder”) before the oven was even lit, so the name stuck.

“A Neapolitan pizza is only four ingredients and a fire. There’s nowhere to hide, so every one of them has to be right.”

Lucia Moretti, chef & co-owner

We opened in the spring of 2014 with six pizzas, one pasta and a borrowed espresso machine. Today the menu is longer and the pasta station runs from 6 a.m., but the dough is the same one Lucia brought from Italy, fed every single day for over a decade.

From flour to fire

What happens in the 50 hours before your pizza

  1. Day 1 · 9 a.m.

    Mix

    Italian 00 flour, cold water, Oregon sea salt and a spoonful of our starter. Nothing else, ever.

  2. Day 1 to 3

    Rest

    Forty-eight hours in the walk-in at 38°F. The slow ferment builds flavor and makes the crust light and easy to digest.

  3. Day 3 · 11 a.m.

    Ball

    Cut and shaped by hand into 250-gram balls, then left to relax for four hours so it stretches without tearing.

  4. When you order

    Stretch & top

    Pressed out by hand, never with a rolling pin, so the air stays in the edge. That’s where the leopard spots come from.

  5. 90 seconds

    Bake

    Straight onto the 900°F oven floor, turned once with a peel, and out to your table while it’s still crackling.

What goes in

Short list, long care

We buy Italian where Italy does it best and Oregon where the farm is down the road.

  • Tomatoes

    San Marzano DOP from the volcanic soil of Campania, crushed by hand each morning with a little salt. That’s the whole sauce.

  • Mozzarella

    Fior di latte we pull ourselves from Oregon curd before every lunch service. It never sees a fridge for more than a day.

  • Oak

    Oregon white oak, seasoned for a year, from orchard trimmings and forest thinning. It burns hot and clean with a gentle smoke.

  • Produce & eggs

    Greens from a family farm on Sauvie Island and pasture-raised eggs from the Willamette Valley. The menu changes when their seasons do.

The oven weighs 6,000 pounds

It was built by hand in Naples from volcanic brick, shipped over in one piece, and lowered through our front window by crane in March 2014. It hasn’t fully cooled down since.

The pasta is rolled at 6 a.m.

Before anyone else is in, our pasta cook rolls egg dough for tagliatelle, pinches agnolotti and pushes rigatoni through bronze dies. The rough surface it leaves is what makes sauce cling.

Good neighbors

Part of the block, not just on it

  • Pizza for the pantry. Every Tuesday, 10% of sales go to a Southeast Portland community food pantry.
  • Fair pay. Everyone on our team earns a living wage, and tips are shared between the dining room and the kitchen.
  • Less waste. Oven ash goes to a community garden, and our takeout packaging is compostable.
